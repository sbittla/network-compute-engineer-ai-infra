# R1 — Two ways the readiness score could be systematically wrong: model answer

The score is `[MODELED]`: the lab applies caps and discounts, but the inputs are still the learner's own
evidence file (Sections 24.2 and 24.9).

1. **Self-report bias (over-claiming).** Levels are chosen by the learner; the evidence rules only cap them.
   The manuscript's pitfall shows the size: the sample learner's claimed AI-network levels average 3.1,
   demonstrated levels 2.5, a 0.6-level gap an interviewer finds in the first deep-dive question.
   *Correction:* a peer or manager reviews the artifacts against Table 24.2 and re-scores; a mock interview
   on the highest-claimed competency.
2. **Matrix mismatch with the target employer.** The teaching weights and targets are a synthesis; an
   employer's ladder may weight automation or inference far more. *Correction:* map the employer's published
   ladder onto the 0–4 scale, or ask an engineer at that level which competencies their loop probes.
3. **Technology drift** (an acceptable alternative). Evidence from a previous generation (for example
   InfiniBand-only work for a RoCEv2 role) passes the 1,095-day stale rule yet transfers poorly.
   *Correction:* a current hardware-backed lab with a manifest.

**Marking.** Each error must have a direction and a named, external source of correction; "be honest with
yourself" earns nothing.
