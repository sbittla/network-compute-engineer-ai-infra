One cluster. Every layer.

A large training job runs only as fast as its slowest GPU, link or disk. A thermally throttled GPU, a swapped cable, a lossy switch queue, a NIC on the wrong PCIe switch and a bad scheduling decision can all produce the same symptom: the step got slower. Most books cover one layer. This one treats AI infrastructure as one connected system and teaches you to follow a workload from the GPU die to the data-center feed.

Every chapter applies the same diagnostic method: observe the symptom, collect evidence, trace the bottleneck across compute, network, storage and orchestration, find the root cause, make a safe change, and validate it with repeatable measurements.

Inside the book

  • 24 chapters in 6 parts: foundations, accelerated compute, networking for AI clusters, distributed AI systems, production operations, and applied engineering
  • One reference cluster, ORION: 256 H100-class GPUs on a rail-optimized 400G RoCE fabric, so every number can be traced from one layer to the next
  • A worked example, a troubleshooting scenario and operating notes in every chapter, with thresholds, safe-change rules and safety boundaries
  • Five appendices: the full reference configuration, a benchmark reporting standard, a 69-point readiness checklist, a glossary, and a dated register of version-sensitive vendor claims
  • A companion repository with 32 labs and 484 exercises: 24 chapter labs and 8 capstones. Most run on a laptop; the extensions run on one real GPU

You will learn to

  • Read GPU, NVLink, NVSwitch and PCIe topology, and the host signals that predict trouble
  • Design rail-optimized RoCE and InfiniBand fabrics: PFC, ECN, DCQCN, buffers, optics and FEC
  • Lay out distributed training and reason about NCCL collectives, PXN and checkpoint cost
  • Size LLM serving: KV-cache capacity, continuous batching, time to first token and inter-token latency
  • Accept and run GPU fleets with burn-in, Kubernetes and Slurm scheduling, SLOs and incident response
  • Change production safely with canaries, staged rollouts and proven rollbacks
  • Plan power, cooling, capacity and the true cost of a useful GPU-hour
  • Secure multi-tenant GPU clusters, from MIG and RDMA isolation to a full threat model

Honest about its numbers. Every quantitative claim is labeled measured, modeled, simulated, illustrative or vendor-specified, and version-sensitive details carry the release and the date they were checked. The book never presents a model as a measurement.

Who it's for: infrastructure, network and platform engineers moving into AI; ML engineers who need to understand the cluster under their jobs; SREs who run GPU fleets; and architects who size, buy and operate them. You need Linux and basic networking. You don't need a GPU cluster to start.
